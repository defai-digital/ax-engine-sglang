Corrective targets destroy the fixed coarse-to-fine semantics: level-i becomes "fix earlier mistakes," a moving, error-dependent target that is harder to learn and hides the real problem. Remaining failures: multimodal continuations force an early deterministic mode commitment in 4 codes with 4% codebook usage; one wrong L0 code poisons all 32 tokens; off-manifold residuals make "nearest code" noisy.

2. No, a discrete global plan is poorly matched to greedy exact-match drafting. NCM optimizes semantic coherence, not token identity. Entropy of a 32-token block summary is much higher than entropy of token t+1. You are asking a 6-layer decoder to solve the harder problem first, then conditioning the easy early tokens on that noisy guess. Drop rule from the running ablation: kill the plan unless plan-mode free-running accepted prefix / draft-ms beats direct one-pass by >=20% at 2k steps. If direct >= plan on accepted length at lower latency, or code accuracy stays <30%, drop it. Do not use teacher-forced token accuracy.

3. Ranked: (1) Left-to-right dependence + prefix-weighted loss, jointly. Acceptance is all about positions 0-3; uniform loss over 32 parallel tokens wastes capacity on positions never reached. Weight loss ~exp(-pos/tau) and spend passes early. (2) Verifier-logit distillation (top-k KL) instead of hard tokens. Hard greedy labels discard uncertainty; KL improves exact match on ambiguous early positions and enables calibrated early-exit. (3) Confidence-based variable draft length. Mask-predict refinement and continuous plans are lower priority until (1)-(2) work.

Chunk-mode no-exposure-bias argument is largely sound and is the key advantage over codes: in greedy speculative decoding, drafts after the first mismatch are discarded, so teacher-forcing on gold prefix is the correct conditioning for the accepted prefix. Caveat: it still wastes capacity training late positions on gold prefixes rarely reached at inference, and calibration for early exit still needs rollout. [2,2,4,8,16] is a bad schedule: 5 passes, with a full pass for positions 16-31 that will almost never be accepted. Use [1,1,2,4] or [2,2,4] for ~8 drafts, then extend adaptively only if confident. 7. Give most budget to chunk mode. It is the only option aligned with the prefix exact-match objective: errors do not poison earlier tokens, teacher forcing is valid, dependence is spent where acceptance mass is, and it composes with prefix loss, KL, and early exit. Direct one-pass keeps independence pathology; plan-mode bets on predicting the hardest thing first through a collapsed codebook.
