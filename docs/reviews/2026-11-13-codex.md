1. **Sound direction; full conversion remains high-risk research.**

Blockwise generation addresses open-ended length, but **K passes versus B passes does not imply B/K speedup**: each refinement processes multiple positions, followed by decoding and causal context commitment. NCM’s published speedups establish performance against diffusion baselines, not optimized Qwen inference. [NCM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14162v1)

Biggest risks:

- **Reconstruction:** 99.9% token accuracy is inadequate for unverified code. Assuming independent errors, a 256-token block is exact only \(0.999^{256}\approx77.4\%\) of the time. Measure exact blocks, identifiers, literals, whitespace, and execution outcomes.
- **Quality:** parallel code prediction imposes conditional independence within levels. Better reconstruction cannot fix a generator that misses correlated decisions.
- **Exposure bias:** training on clean preceding blocks and ground-truth coarse codes mismatches generated histories. Conditioning corruption helps; train and evaluate multi-block rollouts.
- **DeltaNet:** reversing pretrained weights does not produce a pretrained backward model. It changes the operator, convolution boundaries, and hidden-state distribution.
- **State correctness:** refinement states cannot become committed text states. Replay accepted text causally; rejection requires restoring recurrent state, convolution buffers, and KV cache.
- **Verification:** retaining the LM head does not preserve original Qwen quality after joint fine-tuning.

2. **Prefer a separate causal context model and small bidirectional block decoder.**

Keep Qwen’s causal operators intact. Condition a dense NCM decoder through cross-attention to cached context representations or learned summaries; benchmark whether summaries lose long-range identifier information. Reuse embeddings and distill hidden states/logits before attempting wholesale backbone reuse.

Start with **B=32–64**, then test 128; sweep K=4/6/8 against acceptance and latency. Adapt block length to uncertainty and stop markers. Larger blocks worsen first-token latency and wasted verification.

Keep Qwen token IDs initially. Compare VQ against direct-token refinement and a hierarchy ending in token-level prediction. Literal/escape channels can preserve rare strings, but their generation cost must count.

Correct the mask specification: NCM’s block-diagonal mask separates **levels**; cumulative reconstruction already carries coarser information. Explicit cross-level attention is a separate, more expensive ablation. Same-level inputs must exclude target codes. [Attention ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14162v1#A6.SS2.SSS1)

If testing backward DeltaNet, use a separately initialized boundary state and a gated branch; reset between levels. Dense block attention is the cleaner first experiment.

3. **Track A first—but qualify “lossless.”**

Greedy verification can preserve target greedy decoding through prefix matching and target correction.

Exact stochastic speculative sampling normally needs token-conditional proposal probabilities and rejection correction. **Latent-code probabilities are not decoded-token probabilities**: decoding can be many-to-one, requiring marginalization. Deterministic-draft rejection is possible, usually with lower acceptance; otherwise specify and prove an exact coupling. [Speculative decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)

Benchmark against ordinary AR drafting and Qwen’s MTP. A 0.5–1B dense drafter taking 8–12 passes may be expensive beside a target with approximately 3B active parameters. Track B proceeds only after demonstrating useful parallel prediction and favorable economics.

4. **Memory looks plausible; runtime estimates are conditional.**

A conventional 16-byte/parameter mixed-precision Adam budget gives **576GB for 36B parameters**. FP32 gradients can raise this to 648GB. Neither includes activations, communication buffers, gathered parameters, or new modules. Eight H200s provide 1,128GB aggregate; eight B200s provide 1,440GB. Fit requires measured per-rank peaks and a compatible expert/sharding implementation. [H200 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/), [B200 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-b200/)

Your arithmetic is reasonable:

\[
30{\rm B}\times3\times6\times3.5{\rm B}=1.89\times10^{21}\ {\rm FLOPs}.
\]

At 25% utilization of **dense BF16** peaks, approximately 11 H200 days or 5 B200 days follows. MoE sparsity does not qualify for advertised hardware structured-sparsity peaks.

The uncertain assumptions dominate: square schedules imply roughly 3.2–4.5× positions for K=8–12; add context processing, AR loss, checkpoint recomputation, tokenizer training, and teacher generation. Small expert GEMMs and communication may depress utilization.

**Start on H200 unless B200 passes qualification.** FLA issue #913 reports shape-dependent sm_100 failures and silent NaNs; its stale closure does not establish a fix. Test actual forward/backward shapes, gradients, and cached decoding. [FLA report](https://github.com/fla-org/flash-linear-attention/issues/913)

Both support FP8; Blackwell also supports NVFP4 training recipes. Establish BF16 correctness before lower precision; keep sensitive recurrence/router operations appropriately precise. [Transformer Engine](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/transformer-engine/getting_started/index.html)

On RunPod, verify same-host topology, NVLink/P2P, NCCL bandwidth, available capacity, checkpoint storage, and measured dollars per token before committing.

5. **Phased gates**

- **Baseline:** optimized Qwen AR/MTP; measure latency, throughput, cost, and code/math quality across context lengths and concurrency.
- **Tokenizer:** held-out repositories and adversarial literals. Proposed unverified-code gate: ≥99.99% exact blocks, with error confidence bounds and execution parity. Failure redirects to verified drafting.
- **Small Track A pilot:** train on 100M–1B tokens before scaling. Require ≥1.5× end-to-end speedup, including drafting, verification, rollback, and commitment, against the strongest baseline.
- **Correctness:** exact greedy agreement; stochastic distribution tests supplement a correctness proof. Exercise rejection at every block position.
- **Track B pilot:** adapters, then limited unfreezing; generated-history training and AR replay. Proposed gate: ≤1 percentage-point code/math regression, no growing long-rollout deficit, and ≥1.5× speedup.
- **Scale only after gates pass:** measured learning curves determine the 20–50B-token budget. Defer RL until architecture and decoding are stable.
